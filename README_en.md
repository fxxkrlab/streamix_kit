The RC27 specifications are additive over RC26: the player subset grows from 120 to 121 operations and the full specification from 389 to 390; apart from `info.version` both documents change the same 38 places, with no administration change. There are three additions. Login refresh: `POST /sessions` adds the required `expires_at` (the access token expires 24 hours later by default) and the required, nullable `refresh_token` (`stx_sxls_` plus 64 lowercase hex digits, shown once, rotating) and `refresh_expires_at` (30 days, renewed by each refresh, never more than 365 days after the password login). `POST /sessions/refresh` accepts that token besides the personal credential secret `stx_sxrt_`, and its 200 body becomes `oneOf`: `OwnCredentialRefreshed` for a personal secret as before, or the new `SessionRefreshed` for a login refresh (a new 24-hour `access_token` with `expires_at` and `login_id`, plus the next `refresh_token` of the chain and its `refresh_expires_at`). The presented token is spent; presenting it again within 60 seconds while its successor is unused is answered again (a lost response), and any other reuse revokes the chain and every login it minted. Refresh one request at a time and store the new `refresh_token` before using the new access token. Removal list: the new `GET /media/removed?since=` lists the works that left the catalogue at or after `since` (inclusive), oldest first (`MediaRemovalPage`: `data` rows of `id`, `removed_at` and `reason`, `deleted` for deleted or merged into another work and `unavailable` for no current file, plus `next_cursor` and `tracked_since`), with `limit` 1 to 5000 (default 1000) and a cursor bound to the account, the login and `since`. A `since` before `tracked_since` (no sweep yet, or older than the retention) is 410 `removed_history_unavailable` (`MediaRemovalHistoryUnavailable`, with `tracked_since`): reconcile once with `GET /media/ids`, then continue from that response's `Date`. Rows are kept 30 days; visibility follows the work's content domain (adult or not), not library rules or grants, so ignore unknown IDs. The recommended loop is `GET /media/removed?since=<previous Date minus 10 minutes>`, drop those works, then `GET /media?updated_since=<the same instant>`. Logo languages: `Media` adds the required `logos` (at most 50 `{lang, w, h}`, one per stored TMDB logo language, in the order the server would choose them for the request's Accept-Language; `lang` is `xx` for no text, `zh`, a regional Chinese such as `zh-CN` or `zh-TW`, or an ISO 639-1 code), and `GET` / `HEAD /media/{id}/images/{kind}` add the `language` parameter (`kind=logo` only, a BCP 47 tag of 1 to 35 bytes), answered with `Vary: Accept-Language` and, when the language is stored, `Content-Language`. Capabilities add the required `features.logo_languages`, `session_refresh` and `media_removed` (always true) and the limits `media_removed_page_size_max` (5000) and `media_removed_retention_days` (30). Check `/capabilities` before using them; a server at 0.9.0-rc.26 or earlier has none of these capabilities, fields or operations. Earlier, the RC25 specifications are additive over RC24: the player subset grows from 118 to 120 operations and the full specification from 387 to 389; apart from `info.version` the player subset changes 28 places, and the full specification changes 27 more in administration operations (library and user download settings, not published). The additions are the download permission and preload grants. `POST /prefetch/grants` asks before a preload, a whole-file cache or a download: the body `PrefetchGrantRequest` holds `media_id` (the work or the episode), `version_id` (a current file-backed version of it), `category` (`detail` for a detail-page or episode-page preload, `next_episode` for the next-episode preload, `cache` for a whole-file disk cache while playing, `download` for offline viewing) and `network` (`lan` or `wan`, recorded only), and allows no other members. Approval and refusal are both 200 (`PrefetchGrantDecision`, told apart by `granted`). An approval (`PrefetchGrantApproved`) carries `grant_id`, `max_bytes` (the bytes a `detail` or `next_episode` grant may read, 256 MiB by default, between 1 MiB and 10 GiB; null for `cache` and `download`, which are not counted), `max_seconds` (always null) and `expires_at` (the first stream read must start within 10 minutes of issue); a `download` approval adds the offline licence `license_expires_at` (30 days by default) and `renew_after` (half the licence). A refusal (`PrefetchGrantRefused`) carries `reason`: for `cache` and `download` the result of the three-tier download permission (site, library, user), `disabled_globally`, `not_opted_in`, `library_not_allowed` or `user_not_allowed`; for `detail` and `next_episode` `prefetch_disabled` (preloads are off) or `server_busy` (preload capacity is full); for any category `rate_limited` (at most 30 grants per user per minute); `server_busy` and `rate_limited` add `retry_after_seconds`. An invisible item or a version that is not a current file of it is 404 `not_found` and an invalid body 400; neither is a refusal. Send the request as an ordinary request, with no User-Agent purpose product and no `Sec-Purpose: prefetch`; a request marked as a preload meets the preload switch and slots first (403 `prefetch_disabled`, 429 `prefetch_busy`). `POST /prefetch/grants/{grant_id}/renew` renews the offline licence of the caller's `download` grant and decides the download permission again: a 200 approval with new `license_expires_at` and `renew_after` (same `grant_id` and `expires_at`) or a 200 refusal; another user's or an unknown grant, or an item no longer visible, is 404, and any other category is 409 `grant_not_renewable`. Without renewal the client stops offline playback of the copy when the licence ends and says why; the server deletes nothing remotely. `GET` and `HEAD` of `/playback/sessions/{id}/stream` and of disc file reads accept the optional header `STREAMIX-Grant: <grant_id>` (exactly one value); the grant must belong to the session's user and version: a present but invalid grant is 403 `grant_invalid` (never ordinary playback), and a first read after `expires_at` or after the offline licence is 403 `grant_expired`. A `detail` or `next_episode` read reserves what it serves from `max_bytes`: a Range longer than the remaining budget is cut to it and answered as a shorter 206 (trust `Content-Range`), a read without Range is served only when the whole file fits, and with nothing left the answer is 403 `grant_exhausted` without `Content-Range` (416 still means only a range outside the file); such reads take the preload lease outside the concurrent-stream limit whatever the User-Agent purpose, and return 403 `prefetch_disabled` once preloads are off. A `cache` or `download` read re-runs the download permission every time, answers a refusal with 403 and the reason as the error code, and is not counted. The User-Agent gains the purpose product `Streamix-Download/1` for whole-file disk caching and downloads (sent without `Sec-Purpose`); it is never a preload, and a read carrying it re-runs the download permission even without the grant header; when several purpose products appear the order is `Streamix-Play`, then `Streamix-Preload`, then `Streamix-Download`. Capabilities gain the required `features.download_permissions` (always true) and `features.prefetch_grants` (true when the server checks grants; false in Nimbus private mode until the private runtime checks them, when private stream reads check neither `STREAMIX-Grant` nor the download permission and `cache` or `download` is decided only at issue and renewal), plus the required `limits.prefetch_max_bytes_ceiling` (10737418240, 10 GiB); the new schemas are `PrefetchGrantRequest`, `PrefetchGrantDecision`, `PrefetchGrantApproved` and `PrefetchGrantRefused`. Normal playback, its buffering and a cache of only the watched parts need no grant. Check `features.prefetch_grants` first: an RC24 or earlier server has neither capability nor operation, so keep the earlier preload rules there. See [preload grants and downloads](docs/downloads.md). A new schema `VersionDisplay` is added, and `Version` and `BestVersion` gain `display`, a required, nullable server-built short version name. For a version the server records as adult origin, the name is built from the code in the file name (normalized, for example `ABCD-123`), its tags (Chinese or English subtitles, uncensored, leak, decensored, AI decensored, VR, 4K/8K, frame rate, HDR, part CDn) and the technical differences that tell it apart from the other versions of the same work and episode; without a code it falls back to a short title taken from the file name, then the technical facts, then `版本 N` (version N), and it is never the whole file name. `display.text` is the name to show, already unique among the versions of the same work and episode, at most 40 characters and 40 columns (an East Asian character counts two); `basis` says where it came from (`code`, `title`, `technical` or `ordinal`); `code` and `family` hold the normalized code and its family only when `basis` is `code`; `tags` lists the machine names of the tags in `text` in display order (for example `zh_sub`, `uncensored`, `leak`, `4k`, `cd<N>`); `rules_version` names the rules file that produced the name (`YYYY.MM.patch`). Ordinary versions return null and keep their client-built labels. Adult works now list their versions in the adult order: Chinese subtitles first, then leak > uncensored > decensored > AI decensored, then resolution, then bitrate (the weights live in the server's versioned rules file); the parts of one variant stay together in part order and an episode group keeps the place of its first entry; pages of an adult work are cut from that order (the cursor carries the position), and a work with more than 2000 versions keeps the quality order. `best_version` is the first entry of the adult order, and `BestVersion.display` is that version's `Version.display`. UDP client networking is not implemented here; deployment and real-LAN acceptance remain separate.

The Rust candidate models login, browsing, versions, prepared playback sessions, ticket renewal, a bounded byte range, progress and explicit session termination. Video requests use `X-SXAPI-Stream-Ticket`; subtitles additionally retain the original login Bearer. Streamix Server currently serves the original file without transcoding. Players must declare their actual supported formats.

TypeScript, Swift and Kotlin SDKs are unimplemented future work. No packages for those languages are advertised or published.
